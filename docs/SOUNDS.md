# Sounds in Minhad

Generated by `tools/sounds/build_library.py`; do not edit by hand (change the script and run it again).

Minhad bundles the set the owner chose: an Adhan voice per place, each whole and as its opening (exactly the first two takbirs, “Allahu Akbar, Allahu Akbar”, since 0.26), and five short alert sounds (0.26: beeps and birds, the owner's choice of 27 November 2026, replacing 0.18's five recordings of nature). Every sound is a real recording under a licence that allows redistribution and commercial use (public domain, CC0, CC BY or CC BY-SA), or the owner's own; nothing is generated or synthesised. `tools/sounds/fetch_commons.py` re-checks every source (licence on Commons, SHA-1 everywhere) before use. The default Adhan's opening is also the backup alert sound (`alerts/src/main/res/raw/noor_backup_adhan.ogg`, byte for byte the library file).

### How the openings were cut (0.26, 27 November 2026)

The owner: “the short Adhan is just the same version as the Adhan you chose, but it just says the first Allahu Akbar, Allahu Akbar. That's it.” Each opening was found in its original recording from the voice band's energy (250–2500 Hz, 50 ms frames, `tools/sounds/README.md`), spectrograms that were looked at, and a speech recogniser (faster-whisper, model “medium”, Arabic, word times; its times are approximate, the phrase boundaries come from the energy). Each opening starts with a fade of at most 0.2 s just before the voice and ends with a 0.25–0.3 s fade in the pause after the second takbir.

- **Makkah**: 114.45–128.0 s of the original. The muezzin sings both takbirs in one breath, 114.65-127.1 s; the recogniser heard "Allahu Akbar" twice in it (about 119-122 s and 124-127 s) and twice again in the next breath (130.8-144.2 s). The opening ends in the 3.7 s pause between the two breaths, where only the crowd and the Haram's echo are heard.
- **Madinah**: 0.9–18.5 s of the original. One breath, 1.05-15.9 s, then the mosque's echo dies away until about 19.5 s; the recogniser heard "الله أكبر الله أكبر" in it, and again in the next breath (20.4-33.9 s). The opening ends in the echo's tail, 30 dB below the voice.
- **Egypt**: 90.1–96.1 s of the original. The first two takbirs are sung quickly, 90.3-95.7 s (the recogniser, on that phrase alone: "الله أكبر الله أكبر", word confidence 0.6-1.0); after a 0.8 s pause the second two are drawn out over 16 s (96.5-112.5 s), and the first shahada begins at 116.6 s. Until 0.25 the opening held all four takbirs (90.0-112.6 s).
- **Dubai**: 0.76–12.8 s of the original. One breath, 0.8-11.3 s, its echo fading until 13.1 s; the recogniser heard "الله أكبر الله أكبر" in it and in the next breath (13.2-23.6 s), then the two shahadas. The opening ends in the echo, 25 dB below the voice.
- **Dubai (Fajr)**: 0.08–11.85 s of the original. One breath, 0.15-10.3 s, its echo fading until 11.9 s; the recogniser heard "الله أكبر الله أكبر" in it and in the next breath (12.0-20.6 s), then the two shahadas.

## Alert sounds (0.26)

Short real recordings to announce the prayer time: beeps of real devices and birds. No music, melody or musical instrument, nothing synthesised. Each starts at once, ends cleanly and is levelled to about −17 LUFS (true peak at most −1.5 dBTP), close to the Adhans' −16 LUFS. There is one version of each (they are 2–4 s long: no separate short version); an alarm plays it over and over.

Still to do (0.26): the owner asked for five, beeps (“beep beep”) and birds; four birds are here. The beeps chosen on Wikimedia Commons could not be downloaded while 0.26 was made (Commons' file host answered “429 Too Many Requests” to the cloud session for more than two hours; a request was never sent around the limit). Candidates, licences verified on Commons' API, to be listened to (as spectrograms) before use: *Microwave oven.ogg* (stephan, PDSounds, public domain: a real microwave that “finishes with 3 beeps”), *Unlocking a car.ogg* (hc, PDSounds, public domain: a car's remote lock beeping twice, recorded through a window; the upload repeats the two beeps, so only the first two can be used), *Alarmclock-bleeps.ogg* (Geckoerer, CC BY-SA 4.0, “a typical modern alarm clock”: whether it is a recording must be checked; a perfectly clean tone with no room sound would be a generated one) and *Alarm clock - 01.ogg* (Mathieu Kappler, CC BY-SA 4.0, a ringing alarm clock recorded in France). Rejected from their descriptions: *Alarm or siren.ogg* and *Beep alarm clock.ogg* (generated), *Joseph Sardin - Passer domesticus tschilp call* (a toy sparrow), *Alarm Clock (Directory.Audio)* (unknown origin), *Heart Monitor Beep* (probably generated), *Smoke alarm.ogg* (harsh). Until a beep is added, `fx.rain` plays the nuthatch, whose calls sound like beeps.
