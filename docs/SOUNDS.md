# Sounds in Noor

Generated by `tools/sounds/build_library.py`, except the Dubai and Egypt voices (0.18), which were added by hand with the same edits; before the script is run again, they must be added to its `ADHANS`.

Since 0.17 Noor bundles only the set the owner chose on 16 November 2026: an Adhan voice per place, each whole and as its opening (“Allahu Akbar, Allahu Akbar”), and five recordings of nature, each long and short. Every sound is a real recording under a licence that allows redistribution and commercial use (public domain, CC0, CC BY or CC BY-SA); nothing is generated or synthesised. `tools/sounds/fetch_commons.py` re-checks every source (licence on Commons, SHA-1 everywhere) before use. The default Adhan's opening is also the backup alert sound (`alerts/src/main/res/raw/noor_backup_adhan.ogg`, byte for byte the library file).

## Places without a recording yet

These voices are offered as “add your own recording”.

- **Al-Quds** (القدس): No freely licensed recording of the Adhan of al-Masjid al-Aqsa was found, and no Jerusalem recording could be downloaded and checked here. The one candidate, the Adhan heard at the Church of the Holy Sepulchre in the Old City (Freesound 191866, Zabuhailo, CC BY 4.0, 109 s), was fetched on GitHub and examined on 16 November 2026: a speech recogniser heard no phrase of the Adhan in it (only, near its end, what sounds like the greeting that closes a prayer), so it is not used. Examined and not usable (16 November 2026): on Wikimedia Commons, a 15-second clip heard from a distance at the Garden Tomb and a 60-second travel video from the Haram (Neollene Films, 2017: too short to hold a whole Adhan); radio aporee field recordings on the Internet Archive from the Golden Gate (2014: the end of a prayer over a loudspeaker), Pisgat Ze'ev (2016: distant calls with gunfire), the Lion's Gate (2015: street sound, no Adhan), the Notre Dame rooftop at sunset (2013: several distant mosques, 67 s), Bethlehem (2023: distant, behind voices, 64 s) and Ramallah (2019, CC BY 3.0, Maghrib and Fajr from a window: far away, many mosques at once, and not Jerusalem; a speech recogniser heard no words in either). Freesound's "Palestine muezzin" (nicotep) is CC BY-NC; other uploads on the Internet Archive are broadcast or YouTube copies whose uploader does not hold the rights.

## Considered and not used

- *Adhan, Great Mosque of Mecca – 2013* (CC BY 3.0): clearer than the 2012 recording, but it stops during the 11th of the 12 phrases, so it cannot give the whole Adhan.
- *Time of Adhan, Great Mosque of Mecca – Jan 6, 2018*: starts during the first phrase.
- *Oración Al-Azzan* (Madinah, public domain): holds the opening only; the Prophet's Mosque recording is complete and cleaner.
- Makkah and Madinah Adhans on the Internet Archive marked “public domain” or CC0 (HaramainRecordings and similar uploads): captures of television broadcasts whose uploaders do not hold the rights.
- *Adhan/Azaan (Dubai Mall) – Abdulrahman Al-Hindi* on the Internet Archive: a YouTube copy uploaded by someone who does not hold its rights (the Dubai voice comes from the owner's own files instead).
- Jumeira 3, Dubai (radio aporee, 2011): three mosques calling at once, with waves; not the Dubai Mall Adhan.
- *Call to prayer by Sabah Fakhry.mp3*: tagged public domain, but it is a commercial recording by a singer who died in 2021; the claim is not credible.
- The other Egyptian candidates on Freesound, fetched on GitHub and examined on 16 November 2026 with band-energy profiles and a speech recogniser: Alexandria (CyWeb, CC BY 4.0, 185 s: begins at the first shahada, without the opening takbir), Alexandria (nervousneal, CC0, 54 s: stops at "Hayya 'ala-s-salah"), Al-Azhar Park (CC0, 64 s), Maadi (CC BY 4.0, 74 s) and El Quseir (CC0, 127 s): too far away to be heard clearly (the voice barely 5 to 14 dB above the background; the recogniser heard no words).
- Alert sounds generated from code (`tools/sounds/synthesize.py`, up to 0.16): removed at the owner's word (“no generated sounds”, “zero music”).
- Nature: a rooster in Austin, Texas (heavy background noise); NPS clips of the ocean, rain and chickens (5–7 seconds, low bit rate); thunderstorms from Rocky Mountain National Park (mostly thunder, little rain); sparrows in La Bouille and Berlin (traffic hum).
